Lakshmi Puja

Lakshmi Puja is a sacred Hindu ritual dedicated to Goddess Lakshmi, the goddess of wealth, prosperity, and fortune. Devotees worship Goddess Lakshmi to seek blessings for financial stability, happiness, and success in life.

The puja is especially performed during the festival of Diwali, when devotees invite Goddess Lakshmi into their homes to bless them with prosperity and abundance. It is believed that performing Lakshmi Puja with devotion brings wealth, positivity, and good fortune.

1. Introduction to Lakshmi Puja

Lakshmi Puja is performed to honor Goddess Lakshmi, who symbolizes prosperity, fortune, and abundance. Devotees pray to the goddess to remove poverty and invite prosperity into their homes and businesses.

This puja is commonly performed during Diwali, the festival of lights, when homes and temples are beautifully decorated to welcome Goddess Lakshmi.

2. Essential Puja Samagri (Items Required for the Ritual)

  • Idol or image of Goddess Lakshmi
  • Lotus flowers or fresh flowers
  • Rice grains
  • Turmeric and kumkum
  • Incense sticks and camphor
  • Oil lamps (diyas)
  • Coconut
  • Fruits and sweets
  • Coins or currency notes
  • Betel leaves and betel nuts
  • Milk, honey, and ghee

3. Purpose and Importance of Performing Lakshmi Puja

  • To invite prosperity and wealth into life.
  • To seek blessings for success in business and career.
  • To remove financial difficulties.
  • To bring happiness and peace into the home.
  • To express gratitude for the blessings received.

4. Historical and Religious Significance

According to Hindu mythology, Goddess Lakshmi emerged during the Samudra Manthan (churning of the ocean). She symbolizes wealth, prosperity, beauty, and fortune.

Lakshmi Puja holds special importance during Diwali because it marks the return of Lord Rama to Ayodhya after defeating Ravana, and devotees celebrate by lighting lamps and worshipping Goddess Lakshmi for prosperity.

5. Detailed Overview of the Lakshmi Puja Ritual

The puja begins with cleaning and decorating the house to welcome Goddess Lakshmi. Devotees place the idol of the goddess on a decorated platform and offer flowers, sweets, fruits, and coins.

Mantras and aartis dedicated to Goddess Lakshmi are chanted to seek her blessings. Lamps are lit throughout the home to symbolize the removal of darkness and the arrival of prosperity.

Lakshmi Puja concludes with the distribution of prasad among family members and devotees, symbolizing gratitude and divine blessings.
